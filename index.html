A companion that
stays beside the work

Designing a more useful relationship between Personio’s Assistant and the product around it.

Personio Responsive Intelligence · App-frame redesign and embedded assistant

Customer interviews: April–June 2026 · Project dates: [confirm] · My role and delivery status: [confirm]

The Assistant could help with the work. It could also get in its way.

The original Assistant opened over the product. It took up the space where people needed to read, navigate, and act. In Analytics, it could even cover the blank report template it was meant to help someone create.

Then there was the conversation itself. Moving between product areas could reset the thread. The Assistant felt less like a companion to ongoing work and more like a separate chat people had to keep restarting.

People were losing their place, their thread, and the thing they came to do.

In customer interviews between April and June 2026, I saw the same tension surface in different ways. In an employee-change workflow, removing product navigation made it harder to stay oriented. In Analytics, a route change reset the conversation just when someone expected it to continue.

And when a blank report template appeared only after the Assistant was closed, the person had not thought to close it. I took these as connected problems: keep people oriented, carry the conversation forward, and leave the work itself in view.

How might the Assistant stay close without taking over?

I used that question to set the direction. The Assistant should add help to the page, while changing only what the task actually needs.

That meant keeping navigation and workspace visible, carrying useful conversation context between routes, and supporting a workflow from start to finish—not only answering a prompt.

A persistent panel solved a different problem than a better overlay.

I compared three directions: keep the overlay, put AI directly into individual pages, or give the Assistant an embedded panel in the product frame. The overlay kept the interruptions that prompted the work.

Page-level AI made sense for focused tasks, but did not offer one conversation that could travel across the product. The embedded panel offered that shared space, while leaving room for page-native help when a chat was not the right fit.

One Assistant, with room to change shape.

I shaped the experience around the space available. On wide screens, a resizable panel sits beside the page, so people can use the Assistant and still see their work.

On narrow screens, it becomes full-screen to keep the conversation legible. For more involved work, a dedicated /assistant destination gives the same conversation room to grow.

The conversation should not start over when the page changes.

I kept the Assistant close through a persistent entry in the top navigation. Compact navigation gives the page more room, then expands to reveal its labels. As people move between product areas, the conversation stays with them and the page can offer a relevant starting point.

What the Assistant can actually read from a page is a separate design and engineering question. Context, permissions, sensitive or unsaved state, and fallback behavior all need validation before promising what it knows.

Not every AI interaction needs to become a chat.

I also wanted the frame to support more than a single chat window. Specialist widgets could handle agent work, while a focused action—like a summary inside a product area—could use a shared pattern without starting a conversation.

Product teams would still own their domain data and agents. The shared frame provides a coherent place for these experiences to meet; the task should determine which one appears.

Make the measures answer the original problem.

The brief proposes tracking Assistant CSAT, weekly active users, context-loss events, movement between panel and full-screen, and assisted task completion. Support volume, page performance, and time-to-interactive are proposed guardrails.

These are measures to validate, not results. No post-launch data was provided, so there is no impact claim here.

The direction was a frame that made room for the work.

I came back to the original tension throughout the project: help should be close at hand, but it should not make the product disappear. That led me toward a persistent, responsive companion and a broader frame that could also support more focused AI experiences.

I would judge the direction against the same moments that exposed the problem: can people stay oriented, keep their conversation, and reach the work in front of them?
